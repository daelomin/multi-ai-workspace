### Corrections I accept to my own proposal

- **“WhatsApp not advertised” and “video not advertised” were wrong.** The official security page names WhatsApp Business as a connector, and the peers' changelog evidence advertises image/video creation in v0.75.2 (peer-verified; I did not re-fetch the changelog). The correct statement is: the original product advertises these capabilities; whether they survive local routing or are usable as before is unverified. This strengthens option 1's potential migration/connector advantage and weakens one of my specialist arguments.
- **“Option 1 needs no migration” was overstated.** V8 shows an isolated profile. The existing team may be present or absent there, and whether account sync restores it is unknown. CHATGPT-3's correction is accepted.
- **Provider independence is not identical to independence from the application that stores the bots.** CHATGPT is right that a stable vendor app could hold one bot identity while inference changes. My narrower grounds for ranking option 1 third are its limited routes (V8), Cursor-managed model choice and usage (V7), hosted connector custody, and the isolated profile — not a claim that app-held identity is logically impossible.
- **“Akeru's router can be a sidecar module” was too optimistic.** V4 shows no documented per-turn hook and routes fixes to the adapter boundary. The realistic option-3 designs are (a) dispatch a task to a bot variant with the required model, using existing delegation, or (b) edit the adapter/controller path. Per-task routing at dispatch can satisfy the “assess the task, then choose” requirement; mid-task quota switching is a separate, harder problem.
- **My DEEPSEEK-3 over-weighted repository age and stars.** A four-day-old project is a risk, not proof of unsuitability, and `noid-bot`'s reported agent-file import is a directly relevant template-usability feature. It should get a bounded trial, not a dismissal. This changes candidate coverage within option 3, not the option's identity.
- **U4 narrows.** Akeru's channel is the WhatsApp Business Cloud API; the unresolved question is operation coverage and reliability, not the account type.
- **My one-year cost horizon was an assumption, not a HUMAN constraint.** Setup, recurring and unknown costs should be reported separately, as CHATGPT says. I withdraw the horizon as a framing device.

## Challenges to peers

### CHATGPT

**Steelman.** CHATGPT moves the comparison from UI parity to the right structural question: which path stores a named bot and its memory/tools independently of one provider? Its option-3 case is not a feature count. It identifies Akeru's genuinely useful commodity layer (profiles, provider connections, groups, delegation, plugins/MCP), keeps the other options distinct, and correctly treats the shim and reconstruction as weaker foundations rather than merely “proprietary” ones. Its correction of my no-migration claim (CHATGPT-3) is right, its identification of the reconstruction's platform/archive problem (CHATGPT-1) is right, and its auto-deletion finding (CHATGPT-2) is a real adoption constraint. Its test list — actual access, uncertain external writes, good-enough model evaluation, friendly library, connector reality, silence/observer, exit — is the right shape and largely the right depth. Its cost section refuses to invent totals, and its `noid` correction (C8) improves the candidate inventory.

**Strongest objection: “bounded extension” is doing more work than the evidence supports.** CHATGPT's own caveat — “the central extension-cost assumption remains untested” — is not a minor uncertainty; it is the load-bearing assumption behind preferring 3 over 4. The verified routing runbook (V4) documents no per-request/per-turn router hook, says routing must fail rather than fall back, and places model-mapping fixes at the adapter boundary. Akeru's model is saved per bot and resolved per thread (V1, V4), and Akeru “never switches it for you” (V1). Therefore the required capability — “assess whether a model is good enough for each task and route accordingly” — cannot be an unmodified sidecar policy. It must either:
1. **dispatch each task to a differently configured bot** through delegation (existing, but adds a coordinator, a child thread, token overhead, and does not solve mid-task quota exhaustion or external-side-effect reconciliation), or
2. **change the controller/adapter selection path** (a fork or plugin at the boundary), or
3. **change the bot's saved model before the task** (serialises team work and loses the per-task record unless the dispatcher records it).

Akeru's exactly-once guarantee covers delegated-result delivery, not external writes. So a retry on another backend still needs a checkpoint/reconciliation rule that Akeru does not document. CHATGPT's “if coherent extension is practical, option 3 stays first” is the right test but too permissive: coherent extension could still mean edits in three core paths (selection, accounting, skill registration), which is the fork tax that its own strongest-case-against describes.

**Discriminating test.** Take one named bot, one partially completed task, one stub external write accepted before acknowledgement, and a simulated quota failure. Then run a second task that should go to a stronger model. Record:
- which files/configuration change to select a model per task without a human editing the bot;
- whether delegation is used, and what the child thread's usage and failure state look like;
- whether a retry can be prevented from repeating the external write;
- whether the usage view can show the parent and child exclusively, with no double count.

Pass condition: no edits to core orchestration or accounting; a supported adapter/config path or a documented delegation pattern suffices; the parent/child totals are exclusive; the ambiguous write is reconciled or paused. If core orchestration and accounting must both change, the margin over option 4 narrows materially. That is a cost-threshold judgment for HUMAN, not a number I can invent.

### GROK

**Steelman.** GROK's pass is the strongest evidence discipline in the round. It pins repository identities, archive/licence/platform metadata and dates; it distinguishes model APIs, CLI harnesses and subscription logins rather than treating “OpenAI-compatible” as a provider; it verifies that connected-app OAuth stays on Cursor's backend; it corrects the original-video and WhatsApp-connector descriptions; it separates WhatsApp Cloud API messaging from Business administration; it distinguishes Akeru's Observer/Reflector memory stages from HUMAN's effectiveness observer; and it refuses to present an estimate as a bill. Its option-3 case is the right one: use the existing named-bot/profile/group/delegation/MCP substrate and build the differentiators, rather than rebuilding the platform. Its warning that a file existing in `.claude/skills` does not mean the built-in runtime discovers or executes it is exactly the right standard. Its specialist conclusion — no option has native PSTN, Business administration or video — is supported by the evidence it cites, and its acknowledgement that WhatsApp messaging is the one specialist Akeru already wires is fair.

**Strongest objection: the skills fix is underspecified in the same way as the routing fix, and GROK's ranking treats both as projection/policy work.** GROK proposes a “canonical `SKILL.md` library, projected into the directories Claude Code, Codex, and Cursor actually scan” (its preferred-choice item 1). But its own evidence, which I verified, says Akeru's built-in subscription runtime needs no provider CLI installed (V2), while the composer doc says a Claude skill selection is sent as a trailing `/name` “so Claude Code runs that skill” and Codex reads `$name` natively. The docs do not say the built-in runtime scans `.claude/skills` or `.agents/skills`; the skill list is “the skills of the provider the chat is using.” These statements can be reconciled in more than one way:
1. Akeru has its own catalog for built-in drivers, and directory projection is invisible to the menu;
2. Akeru runs a bundled Claude Code-like harness for some routes, and the “no CLI” statement means “no separately installed CLI,” not “no harness”;
3. Both mechanisms exist, and the answer differs by driver.

Until the driver is inspected, “project the file” is not a supported plan; it is a hypothesis. This is a decisive difference because HUMAN's skills priority is explicit, and the same ambiguity affects whether a DeepSeek Custom API bot can see a canonical skill at all (its skill list is provider-scoped, and DeepSeek is a Custom API with no documented skill directory). GROK states the risk in its “what would change my mind” item 3 but still ranks option 3 first on a projection assumption. The challenge is not that GROK is wrong to prefer option 3; it is that the effort estimate and the confidence should reflect “adapter work per provider” rather than “one projection layer.”

**Discriminating test.** On a clean Akeru install:
1. Add a unique `SKILL.md` bundle to each documented root (`.claude/skills`, `.agents/skills`, and any Akeru-specific root).
2. For a Claude-built-in bot, a Codex-built-in bot and a DeepSeek Custom API bot, check whether it appears in the `$`/`/` menu and whether invoking it actually loads the instructions and any scripts/dependencies.
3. Inspect the driver code to identify whether discovery is a filesystem scan, a catalog, or a CLI invocation.
4. Repeat after a provider change on the same bot and task.

If the menu only shows a provider catalog and there is no supported registration hook, option 3's neutral-skill requirement needs per-driver changes or a fork. If a provider change silently loses the skill or its dependencies, the “same persistent bot” claim is unmet for skills even though the profile survives. GROK's item 3 already makes option 4 the fallback if this fails; the test decides whether the fallback is likely.

## Preferred choice

**Current ranking: 3 > 4 > 1 > 2. No revision to the order; a material revision to confidence and conditions.**

**Option 3 remains primary, with Akeru as the candidate to spike and extend.** It is still the only option that already stores a named bot's identity, model, tools and memory outside any one provider, and it supplies groups, delegation, a plugin/MCP substrate, a background service and cross-platform clients under MIT. The verified challenges do not remove that. They do move the required work: per-task routing is a dispatch design or an adapter/controller change, not a documented sidecar (V4); neutral skills may require per-driver registration rather than a file projection (DEEPSEEK-8); and the requested task/parent-child usage attribution is not native (V5). That is more work than my proposal implied, but it is still less than rebuilding the platform.

**Strongest objection to my own choice.** If the continuity, routing, skills and usage spike shows that all four require changes in Akeru's core controller and accounting layers, the retained value is limited to clients, profiles, groups and memory — and a small, well-scoped DIY composition may then be the smaller long-term burden. I do not claim that threshold is crossed on current evidence. The honest position is: option 3 is preferred on current evidence, conditional on a spike that measures the extension surface, not on maintainer documentation alone.

**Runner-up: option 4.** It remains the only path that can implement every priority natively, including a canonical provider-neutral skill library, an auditable per-task route record, checkpoints before external writes, exclusive parent/child usage and a reviewable observer. It loses because it must first rebuild the commodity layer Akeru already supplies. The gap has narrowed, not widened: the verified routing/skills/usage facts show that option 3 does not supply those differentiators for free either.

**Option 1.** Not higher. The official security page is decisive on the primary goal: cloud-only deployment, Cursor-managed model selection, no customer-facing picker, usage recorded as Grok Bot usage rather than by model (V7). The shim adds Codex OAuth and compatible endpoints with an optional quota/auth fallback, not a Claude or DeepSeek subscription route (V8), and it uses an isolated profile (V8). The hosted connector inventory (including WhatsApp Business) is a real short-term advantage, but its retention under the shim is unverified and it does not supply provider-independent identity, per-task routing or accountable usage. **Option 2.** Last. The verified archive, absent licence and macOS 0.18 platform constraints are enough; the missing current Pages/roster/skills evidence compounds them. The absence of a README statement is “unverified”, not “not supported” (DEEPSEEK-9), but it does not change the order.

**Strongest case against the whole ranking.** If DeepSeek tool calls cannot be made reliable through either its API or a harness, then the “four backends” premise is weaker than any option's assessment assumes, and the first decision is not platform selection but backend-route selection. That is why DEEPSEEK-6 is a major finding, not a footnote. It affects 3 and 4 roughly equally, so it does not by itself change 3 versus 4; it does change what “success” means.

**What would change the ranking, as bounded tests:**
1. **Continuity and routing.** A named bot with a persona, memory, one skill and one partially completed task moves Codex → Claude → DeepSeek → Grok and back. Record model/transport/auth, preserved skill and tool set, confirmed work, outstanding ambiguity, and whether the next action repeats an external write. 2. **Skills discovery.** One canonical `SKILL.md` bundle is discoverable and executable on the Claude, Codex and DeepSeek routes through a documented mechanism. 4. **Deletion safety.** Akeru's seven-day archive deletion is disabled by a documented setting or a bounded patch, and a test proves an archived bot survives. If it cannot be disabled without a patch that also has to be maintained, count it in the fork tax under the no-deletion rule.
5. **Exit and restore.** Export the full working state — memory, routines, skill assignments, usage, checkpoints — restore it on a second environment, and verify no data is silently omitted. If only the main archive exists, reversibility is lower than the “local files” story suggests.
6. **Option 1 bridge test.** Run the shim against a copy/isolated profile and check whether the existing team, rooms, pages and routines appear and whether the hosted connectors still work. A positive result makes option 1 a credible short-term bridge; a negative result removes its main advantage. It still does not make it the identity store.
7. **Candidate test.** If `noid-bot`'s agent-file import gives a materially better template-to-instance flow and its experimental substrate proves stable enough, it becomes the option-3 candidate to compare against Akeru. The option remains option 3.

### DEEPSEEK-6 · DeepSeek tool calling is undocumented while the options' DeepSeek routes assume it

- **Section:** HUMAN primary goal (DeepSeek backend); option-1, option-3 and option-4 provider rows in CHATGPT.md, GROK.md and DEEPSEEK.md. Option 2 does not name DeepSeek at all.
- **Type:** evidence gap / dependency risk.
- **Severity:** major.
- **Finding:** The routes that claim DeepSeek support map it to the public API: Akeru's Custom API preset takes an API key/base URL (V2), a DIY adapter would do the same, and the shim's `openai-compatible` route could point at it (V8). The DeepSeek function-calling guide as fetched on 2026-10-24 contains a plain chat example and the model names `deepseek-flash` and `deepseek-v4-pro`; it does not document a `tool_calls` request/response schema, function definitions or streaming tool behaviour. GROK correctly labels DeepSeek tool use unverified, but neither proposal treats it as a decision-critical gap. If the API does not support the tool loop Akeru or a DIY runtime needs, the DeepSeek backend is chat-only and the stated goal — the same persistent bot with skills, procedures and task progress across Codex, Claude, DeepSeek and Grok — is not met for tool-using tasks. The absence of documentation is not proof of absence; that is why this is a test, not a rejection.
- **Proposed correction:** Before treating any option as satisfying the primary goal, run a bounded tool-call round trip on DeepSeek's OpenAI-compatible endpoint and on the Anthropic-compatible endpoint reported by GROK through a Claude Code harness. Record the exact model, streamed tool-call deltas, parallel calls, error mapping and a schema-incompatible retry. If only the harness route works, specify a harness adapter for DeepSeek and label the Custom API route text-only. Do not claim four-backend tool parity from a successful chat completion or a model list.
- **Evidence:** [DeepSeek function-calling guide](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/guides/function_calling), fetched 2026-10-24 (no tool schema); [Akeru providers](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/opencoredev/akeru-bot/main/docs/user/providers.md), 2026-10-24 (Custom API, DeepSeek preset, API key); GROK.md's own “tool use unverified” qualification; DEEPSEEK.md V7. No request was submitted.
- **Confidence:** high that the fetched documentation does not establish tool calling; medium that the capability is absent or incomplete.

### DEEPSEEK-7 · Akeru's seven-day archive deletion has no documented disable and is a hard adoption constraint

- **Section:** Option 3 adoption and cost; CHATGPT-2; workspace no-deletion rule.
- **Type:** operational risk / policy conflict.
- **Severity:** major.
- **Finding:** Akeru's bot guide states: “Akeru deletes an archived bot automatically 7 days after it was archived.” The page describes no setting that disables it. This conflicts directly with the workspace rule that nothing is deleted, trashed or pruned without an explicit message naming the items. CHATGPT-2 correctly raises the conflict, but its proposed correction assumes a disable/removal mechanism that the fetched page does not document. If disabling requires a code patch, that patch is part of the option-3 fork tax and must be verified before adoption; a “reversible” archive action is not reversible if the system later deletes it.
- **Proposed correction:** Verify whether a configuration flag, policy setting, or supported extension disables the timer. If none exists, scope the patch and its maintenance before any trial, and add a test that archives a bot, advances or waits the period, and confirms it survives. Do not archive, delete or prune any bot, image or data in a comparison trial. Treat “no documented disable” as unverified until the source or an issue confirms it.
- **Evidence:** [Akeru bots](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/opencoredev/akeru-bot/main/docs/user/bots.md), fetched 2026-10-24, quotation above; CHATGPT-2; the workspace's explicit no-deletion instruction.
- **Confidence:** high for the documented behaviour and the absence of a documented disable; unknown for whether an undocumented setting or patch exists.

### DEEPSEEK-8 · Akeru's “no CLI needed” subscription runtime conflicts with its Claude Code skill-dispatch description

- **Section:** Option 3 skills; DEEPSEEK.md V11/V18; GROK skills table; CHATGPT C1/C2.
- **Type:** inconsistency / evidence gap.
- **Severity:** major.
- **Finding:** Akeru's providers doc says ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, Kimi and OpenCode Go “run through Akeru's built-in runtime” and “You do not need their command-line tools installed or signed in.” Its composer doc says a Claude skill selection is sent as a trailing `/name` “so Claude Code runs that skill,” and Codex reads `$name` natively. The ambiguity matters in both directions: if the built-in runtime is a bundled harness, “no CLI” means “no separate install,” and projection may work; if it is a provider catalog, projection may be invisible and the neutral-skill priority requires per-driver registration. Akeru's own skill menu, not the presence of a file, is the acceptance evidence.
- **Proposed correction:** Inspect the built-in Claude/Codex drivers and test the same unique `SKILL.md` bundle in each documented root on a Claude-built-in bot, a Codex-built-in bot and a DeepSeek Custom API bot. Record whether the skill appears in the menu, whether invocation loads its instructions and dependencies, and whether a provider change preserves it. - **Evidence:** [Akeru providers](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/opencoredev/akeru-bot/main/docs/user/providers.md) and [composer](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/opencoredev/akeru-bot/main/docs/user/composer.md), both fetched 2026-10-24; CHATGPT C1/C2; GROK's skills table and “what would change my mind” item 3.
